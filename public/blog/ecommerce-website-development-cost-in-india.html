Ecommerce website development cost in India ranges from ₹40,000 for a starter store on a ready-made theme to ₹35,00,000+ for an enterprise platform or multi-vendor marketplace. This guide breaks down every rupee: by store tier, platform, business model, industry, city, features and the running costs that arrive after launch.

The short answer

  • Starter store (theme, up to ~100 products): ₹40,000 – ₹1,50,000. A freelancer or DIY build can start near ₹25,000.
  • Mid-range store (custom design, 100–1,000 products, shipping and GST integrations): ₹1,50,000 – ₹4,00,000. This is where most serious D2C brands land.
  • Advanced custom store (1,000+ SKUs, CRM or ERP, custom checkout): ₹4,00,000 – ₹10,00,000.
  • Enterprise platform, headless build or marketplace: ₹10,00,000 – ₹35,00,000+.
  • Running costs: payment gateway fees of about 2.36% per order (2% + 18% GST on the fee), plus platform, hosting, apps and maintenance of 15–20% of the build cost a year.

Ask five agencies for an ecommerce quote and you can easily get five answers between ₹25,000 and ₹25 lakh. They are not all wrong. A 30-product boutique on Shopify and a multi-vendor marketplace with ERP sync are both "online stores", but they are priced like completely different products.

To build this guide, we compared four detailed Indian ecommerce pricing guides from development agencies, kept the figures they agree on, added what each one covers that the others miss, and checked the numbers against our own builds. Where sources disagree, we show a range. All prices are in Indian rupees (₹), exclude 18% GST unless stated, and reflect 2027 market rates.     Shopify fees in India: pick the plan by your sales

    Shopify Payments is not offered in India, so Indian stores take payments through a third-party gateway such as Razorpay, and Shopify adds its own fee on every order: 2% on Basic, 1% on Grow and 0.6% on Advanced. That changes which plan is cheapest. At the yearly-billed India prices at the time of writing (Basic ₹1,499, Grow ₹5,599 and Advanced ₹22,680 a month), our calculation shows:

    • Below about ₹4.1 lakh in monthly sales, Basic is cheapest.
    • Between ₹4.1 lakh and about ₹43 lakh a month, Grow costs less than Basic, even though its plan fee is almost four times higher.
    • Above about ₹43 lakh a month, Advanced also wins on fees. Below that, choose it only for its features.

    Plan prices change. Check Shopify's India pricing page before you decide, and add app subscriptions (usually ₹3,000 – ₹15,000 a month) to whichever plan you choose.

    WooCommerce

    The plugin is free and there is no platform fee per order, so WooCommerce is usually cheaper to start and cheaper to run at high volume. You pay for hosting, a theme and paid extensions, and you own updates, backups and security. It pairs well with a strong content and SEO strategy. Very large catalogues need better hosting and caching.

    Magento, BigCommerce and headless

    Magento (Adobe Commerce) is built for large catalogues, multi-store setups and complex B2B pricing, with build and hosting costs well above the tiers here. BigCommerce sits between Shopify and Magento, with native multi-currency support that suits brands selling abroad. A headless storefront gives the most speed and design freedom, at a higher build cost. For a first store, we rarely recommend any of the three.

    Custom development

    A custom build costs the most upfront, but has no platform fees and no ceiling on what the store can do. It starts to pay for itself once order volume, catalogue structure or operations stop fitting a hosted platform: for many Indian D2C brands, that is somewhere around ₹50 lakh in monthly sales.

    7India-specific features every store needs

    A store built for Indian buyers needs a few things that global templates do not include by default. Budget for them from the start.

    • UPI, cards, net banking and wallets through an Indian gateway such as Razorpay, PayU, Cashfree or CCAvenue.
    • Cash on delivery (COD) with rules to limit fake orders, such as OTP confirmation or COD fees.
    • GST-compliant invoicing, correct tax calculation by state and e-way bill generation for larger shipments.
    • Indian logistics integration with Shiprocket, Delhivery or Blue Dart for rates, labels, tracking and pin-code serviceability.
    • WhatsApp integration for order updates, abandoned-cart nudges and support, which is where many Indian customers prefer to talk.
    • Mobile-first design and fast pages, since most traffic comes from phones on mixed network speeds.
    • Basic SEO from day one: clean URLs, unique titles and descriptions, product schema and a sitemap.

    8What drives the price up?

    Two stores with the same number of products can be lakhs apart. These are the factors that move a quote most:

    1. Catalogue size and structure. Twenty products is a day of data entry. Five thousand SKUs with variants, nested categories, filters and bulk imports is a project in itself.
    2. Design depth. A theme is cheap. A custom UX costs more, and is usually where gains in add-to-cart and checkout completion come from.
    3. Integrations. Each gateway, courier API, GST tool, CRM or ERP adds build and testing time.
    4. Custom features. Subscriptions, loyalty points, multi-vendor payouts, AI recommendations and product configurators are where budgets climb fastest.
    5. Traffic headroom. Building for Diwali or sale-week spikes costs more upfront, and far less than a store that goes down on its busiest day.
    6. Security and compliance. SSL, PCI-DSS-compliant payment flows, regular audits and GDPR if you sell to Europe.
    7. Who builds it, and where. Freelancer, agency or in-house team, and the city they work from.

    12Hidden costs most store owners miss

    • Product photography and video: ₹20,000 – ₹2,00,000, and it directly affects conversion.
    • Product descriptions and category content: ₹15,000 – ₹1,00,000 for SEO-friendly, original copy.
    • Legal and compliance: terms, privacy, refund and shipping policies and GST advice, ₹10,000 – ₹1,50,000.
    • Customer service tools: live chat, helpdesk and WhatsApp business tools, ₹5,000 – ₹40,000 a year.
    • Analytics and testing: heatmaps and A/B testing beyond Google Analytics, ₹10,000 – ₹80,000 a year.
    • AI and LLM usage: AI chatbots, search and product recommendations bill per use, so costs grow with traffic. Set limits and budget alerts before launch.
    • Platform migration: moving to another platform later can cost ₹2,50,000 – ₹12,00,000+. Choosing well at the start is the cheapest fix.
    • Renewals: domain, hosting, theme and plugin renewals are often priced higher than the first-year offer.
    • GST: 18% on top of most agency quotes, and on gateway fees.

    13Why spending on speed and checkout pays off

    A cheap store that loads slowly or checks out badly loses more in sales than it saved on the quote. Every rupee you spend on ads has to get through the product page and the checkout.

    • Google's research found that 53% of mobile visits are abandoned when a page takes longer than three seconds to load.
    • In a Deloitte study for Google, a 0.1-second speed improvement lifted retail conversions by 8.4%.
    • Portent's analysis found that an ecommerce page loading in 1 second converted about 2.5 times better than one loading in 5 seconds.

    With typical ecommerce conversion rates of 1–3%, a small lift in checkout completion is real money. Spend on speed, mobile checkout and trust signals before adding extra features.

    16How to reduce your ecommerce website cost

    1. Launch an MVP. Start with what earns money: catalogue, cart, checkout and payments. Add wishlists, loyalty and AI recommendations in phase two, funded by sales. This can cut the first build by 30–50%.
    2. Choose the right platform first. A migration later costs far more than picking well now. Most SMBs do not need Magento or a custom build at launch.
    3. Use a premium theme, then brand it. Customising a good theme costs a fraction of a blank-canvas design, as long as it is fast and mobile-first.
    4. Lock the scope in writing. A clear requirements document stops scope creep. Changes made mid-project cost far more than planned ones.
    5. Prepare your own product data. Clean spreadsheets of products, variants, prices and photos save hours of paid work.
    6. Phase the work with milestones. Smaller, defined sprints with milestone-based billing keep costs visible.
    7. Build SEO in from day one. Organic traffic is the cheapest long-term channel and reduces your dependence on ads.
    8. Compare quotes on scope, not price. The cheapest quote often leaves out design, GST setup, testing or support.

    17Future-proofing your store

    • Progressive web app (PWA): ₹50,000 – ₹2,00,000 for an app-like mobile experience without app store fees.
    • Headless commerce: ₹3,00,000 – ₹12,00,000 upfront, but cheaper to change and extend later.
    • API-first build: makes it easy to add marketplaces, apps, POS and new sales channels.
    • Cloud hosting: ₹30,000 – ₹1,50,000 to set up, and scales for sale-day traffic.
    • AI-ready data: clean product data and events let you add AI search, recommendations and chatbots when they make sense.

    19How to get an accurate quote and choose a partner

    Send these three things with your enquiry

    • Catalogue size: a rough product count, categories and variants.
    • Must-have features: gateways, COD, subscriptions, multi-vendor, loyalty, and any CRM or ERP to connect.
    • Design expectation: theme, lightly customised or fully custom.

    Checklist before you sign

    • Review live ecommerce projects in your industry or at your scale.
    • Confirm the quote includes GST setup, gateway and courier integration, testing and product upload.
    • Ask for milestones, milestone-based billing and weekly progress reviews.
    • Confirm who owns the code, theme, accounts and data, and that you get a full handoff.
    • Ask about post-launch support, response times and the maintenance price.
    • Check that speed, mobile checkout and technical SEO are part of the scope.
    • Confirm whether prices include GST.
